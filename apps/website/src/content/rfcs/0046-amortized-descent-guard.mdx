# RFC 0046 — The amortized descent guard (D-28 refined)

Settled 2026-07-12. The D-28 runtime guard bailed on any same-judgment
re-entry whose subject (first argument, by term size) did not strictly
decrease. That over-approximates the D-28 discipline and rejects
correct programs: a match nested in another match's arm body re-enters
`arm_bind` with an unrelated binder-list subject (`#none` vs `#none` —
equal size), even though the derivation is descending fine through
`check_C` on strictly smaller syntax. `binds` (binder'd nesting) and
`op_check` (bundle-in-bundle) have the same shape: their subject
carries the *local* peel, not the global descent.

**The refinement.** Track, per active frame, whether it strictly
descended vs its nearest enclosing same-judgment frame ("flagged").
A non-descending re-entry of judgment J is allowed iff a flagged frame
is active strictly between J's enclosing frame and the new call
(implementation: a counter of active flagged frames, snapshotted at
push, decremented at pop — completed sibling subtrees don't count).
Direct loops (`spin x := spin x`) still bail: nothing flags between
the two frames.

**Why it terminates (for subtree-passing rules).** If every call
passes subtrees of existing terms (the D-28 static discipline),
subject sizes are bounded and every allowed re-entry consumes a fresh
strict descent on the ancestor path; an infinite path would need
infinitely many strict descents by finitely many judgments along one
nested chain — a strictly decreasing unbounded chain, impossible.

**The backstop.** Rules that *construct* growing terms (instantiation
can outgrow the syntax) could in principle alternate two judgments'
flags upward forever. A hard derivation-depth cap (100k frames)
converts any such divergence into a bail, restoring the unconditional
termination guarantee. The cap is far above real derivations.

**Alternative rejected**: declaring per-judgment measure arguments in
the `.type.langue` signature (e.g. measure `arm_bind` by binders +
body). Sound and declarative, but it threads new syntax through the
lexer, parser, AST, codegen, and engine, and every future auxiliary
judgment must remember to annotate; the engine-side refinement fixes
the class.
